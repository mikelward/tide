Settings

The settings panel's newer pages, over the dimmed desktop as it opens. The sidebar lists every page in the panel's order, grouped under Hardware, Windows and General, whose headings can't be chosen; the card grows to fit them.

Keyboard: the keyboards' settings, then their shortcuts below a Shortcuts heading, which openPage keys and the launcher's Keyboard shortcuts scroll to. Keys was a page of its own.

Start at login: the commands the shell runs once a session, through tide launch --background --unless-running, so none takes focus and one running already isn't started again. The arrows move a command and the bin takes it out; one typed at the foot is added with Enter, and one with a quote, backslash or $ is refused, saying why. A change takes effect at the next login.

Notifications: Do not disturb now, on a schedule (from and to a quarter hour a step, and the days a window starts on), and whether critical alerts break through it. The schedule turns Do not disturb on as a window starts and off as it ends, unless you changed it meanwhile. Below, the muted apps and sites: the bin unmutes one, and one typed at the foot is muted with Enter.

Clipboard: whether the history behind Super+V keeps new copies, how many (− and + step through 25 to 1,000), and Clear history, which says how it went.

Power: the battery as the bar's popover shows it, the power profile when power-profiles-daemon is there (the page says so when it isn't), and what closing the lid does with no other monitor: Suspend, logind's own; Lock; or Do nothing, which hold logind's lid switch.

Workspaces: the standing workspaces (the first is home), home's apps and the name patterns, each a list with a bin per entry, arrows where order counts, and a field to add one, refused with the reason when the files wouldn't take it. Then each standing workspace's own settings: a switch for whether its new windows, and launches from it, ask for a workspace (on for home by default), and its pinned apps, which tide's close keys leave open there.

Floating: the rules that float a window, each a class and, optionally, a title pattern, with arrows, a bin and two fields to add one. Below, Floating now: the floating windows no rule covers, one per class. Always float, or Super+Alt+F for the focused window, fills the fields with its class and exact title, the title selected to trim; Enter or Add adds it. More about this window, opened here, lists what else Hyprland says of it, read-only. The second shows a pattern the shell and Hyprland would read differently refused, saying why.

Appearance: the wallpaper rows, each with its swatch, choose button and path. A picture has a preview under its row: the whole picture, a frame for what each size of monitor shows, and what it trims dimmed, with the numbers under it. Here a 3840×2400 picture loses a third of its height on a 3440×1440 monitor, all of it off the bottom with Fill, keep top, and two thirds of its width, equally at each side, on a 1440×2560 one turned on its side; each size of monitor has a preview of its own, which wrap. Each picture has its own position, under its row, stepping through Fill, centered; Fill, keep top; Fill, keep bottom; Fit whole picture; and Stretch to fill. The second shows Stretch for light, the whole picture in the monitor's shape, and Fit for dark: the whole picture, with the bar's color in the bars beside it. A color has no position and no preview. The times and location rows above them are left out.
