Boot splash

From the boot loader to the login screen, tide shows its logo and name on black, with a small spinner below. The black matches the firmware’s screen and the greeter’s first frame, so one follows another without a flash. It is a Plymouth theme on Plymouth’s own two-step plugin, which tide-setup --splash installs and makes the default.

Boot. tide’s two waves, in the bar’s accent blue, and its name sit in the middle of every screen. The spinner below them is in the bar’s dim text color over a faint ring, and turns once a second. It shows from the moment the initramfs starts until greetd’s greeter draws, or the display manager you kept.

Unlock the disk. An encrypted disk asks for its passphrase in place of the spinner, under the logo. Each key typed adds a dot. Under the field are cryptsetup’s prompt, then the keyboard layout the initramfs types in and, when it’s on, Caps Lock. Plymouth fixes that order.

A message. What the boot sends Plymouth to show, such as a disk check’s progress, goes under the spinner. Esc swaps the splash for the boot’s text messages, and back.

tide
Restart and shut down. The same spinner, while services stop. Updates installed at boot (an offline update on Fedora) show a progress bar under it, with Plymouth’s own title.
